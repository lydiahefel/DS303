Risk Factors as Predictors of Cervical Cancer

Prediction of Cervical Cancer Indicators

Lydia Hefel

Chaminade University

2026-05-07

Background

  • ~11, 000 new cases a year in the U.S.

  • Number of new cases declining over the years

  • ~300,000 worldwide and ~4,000 U.S. death annually

  • Labeled the most preventable cancer

  • Screenings (i.e. pap smears) can detect pre-cancerous cells

Research Question

Which risk factors are the best predictors of whether or not a person is diagnosed with cervical cancer?


Why it matters…

  • Most preventable cancer yet 300,000 deaths worldwide each year

  • Understand high risk lifestyle choices

  • Improve detection process

  • Better prevention

Dataset

  • Found on Kaggle - Cervical Cancer Risk Classification

  • Sourced from UCI ML Repository - Cervical Cancer Risk Factors for Biopsy

  • Collected at Hospital Universitario de Caracas in Caracas, Venezuela


Why this dataset?

  • Risk factor classification

  • Publicly available with multiple copies


Key Variables

  • Hypothesis: num_of_pregnancies and first_sexual_intercourse

  • Results: dx_hpv, smokes, and num_of_pregnancies

Data Cleaning Process


project_data <- read_csv(here("data/kag_risk_factors_cervical_cancer.csv"))

project_data <- clean_names(project_data)

project_data <- project_data %>%
  rename_with(~ gsub("st_ds", "stds", .))

colSums(project_data == "?", na.rm = TRUE)

project_data <- project_data %>%
  mutate(across(where(is.character), ~na_if(.,"?")))

project_data <- project_data %>%
  mutate(across(where(is.numeric), as.factor))

project_data <- project_data %>%
  mutate(across(where(is.character), as.numeric))

project_data <- project_data %>%
  mutate(across(c(iud, stds, smokes, hormonal_contraceptives), as.factor))

project_data <- project_data %>%
  mutate(across(c(age), as.numeric))

Findings


HPV diagnosis was the most statistically significant predictor of cervical cancer.


Number of pregnancies and smoking frequency were not significant on their own…

but they became significant when interacting with HPV diagnosis.

Limitations

  • Dataset size

  • Missing data (NA/?)

  • Demographic representation
